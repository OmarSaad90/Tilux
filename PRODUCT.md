# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Mid-to-high budget homeowners considering a tiling renovation (bathroom, kitchen, living space). They browse on a Saturday morning, often on mobile, in natural light, feeling excited but cautious. They want to feel confident they're hiring a premium, reliable craftsman — not just the cheapest option. Their primary question: "Can I trust these people with my home?"

## Product Purpose

Tilux Studio's landing page converts renovation interest into consultation bookings. It exists to establish credibility, showcase craftsmanship, and lower the anxiety of hiring. Success means the visitor ends their scroll session feeling: "These are professionals. I want to contact them."

## Brand Personality

Warm, precise, assured. Knowledgeable without being cold. Like a trusted craftsman who also has impeccable taste in materials.

## Anti-references

- Generic contractor sites: blue/gray color schemes, stock photos of workers in hard hats, "Get a Free Quote!" as the hero CTA. Tilux is not a plumber.
- Dark moody aesthetics: dramatic dark backgrounds, neon accents, nightclub energy. The client has rejected this direction explicitly.
- Discount / bargain feel: cluttered layouts, promotional banners, lots of price talk. Tilux works in a premium tier.
- Over-animated portfolio sites: parallax overload, style over substance, feels like a design school project rather than a business.

## Design Principles

1. **Let the work lead.** Tile photography and before/after evidence are the primary trust-builders. Copy supports; it never competes with the work itself.
2. **Warm expertise.** The voice is knowledgeable and precise, but never cold or salesy. Like a craftsman who loves what they do and knows every material intimately.
3. **Material honesty.** Colors, textures, and surfaces should feel like they belong in a well-designed room, not a tech product or SaaS dashboard.
4. **Readability over decoration.** Every design decision that makes text harder to read must be reconsidered. The tile texture is a feature, not an excuse to sacrifice legibility.
5. **Restraint builds trust.** One strong move per section is enough. Clutter signals uncertainty; clarity signals confidence.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum. Text on all backgrounds must clear 4.5:1 contrast, including on tile-textured surfaces.
- Mobile-first reading order: homeowners browse on phones.
- No motion that can't be reduced: respect `prefers-reduced-motion` on all scroll-triggered animations.
